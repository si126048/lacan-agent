# Discourse Analysis Framework

## Signifier and Signified
Language operates through the chain of signifiers. Each utterance carries meaning beyond its literal content. The subject is constituted through speech.

## Repetition and Return
Repeated signifiers across different contexts reveal unconscious patterns. What returns is not the same - repetition changes meaning across contexts.

## The Subject of the Enunciation
The speaking subject is split between the subject of the statement (what is said) and the subject of enunciation (the act of saying). In group chat, this split manifests in how participants position themselves.

## Desire and Lack
Desire emerges from lack. In conversational dynamics, what is not said often matters more than what is said. Gaps, silences, and withdrawals signal desire.

## The Other
The subject addresses the Other. In group chat, each message is directed at an imagined Other - the group, a specific person, or the symbolic order itself.

## Jouissance
Excessive enjoyment that exceeds pleasure. In chat dynamics, this appears as spam, emoji floods, inside jokes, or ritualistic exchanges that serve no communicative function but sustain group cohesion.

## The Real, Symbolic, Imaginary
- **Symbolic**: The structure of language, rules, group norms
- **Imaginary**: Identification, rivalry, mirror relations between participants
- **Real**: That which resists symbolization - trauma, impossibility, the unspeakable

## Group Dynamics
Groups form around shared signifiers. Leadership emerges through mastery of the group's symbolic order. Exclusion and inclusion operate through linguistic markers.
